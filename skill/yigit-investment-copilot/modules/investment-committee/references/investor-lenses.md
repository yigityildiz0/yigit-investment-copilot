# Investor lenses (paraphrased checklists, adapted for BIST)

These are summaries of widely documented approaches, written in our own words. Use them as structured questions, not as authority. Each lens returns pass / mixed / fail with one reason.

## Value and quality

**Graham — margin of safety.** Is the price well below a conservative estimate of value (earnings power, net assets)? Is the balance sheet strong (current assets comfortably above current liabilities, modest debt)? Are earnings stable and dividends reliable over a long period? Would a sharp price drop be an opportunity rather than a disaster? *BIST:* use inflation-adjusted equity and real earnings; P/B below 1 is common in inflationary markets and is not automatically cheap.

**Buffett / Munger — durable business at a fair price.** Is it inside our circle of competence? Is there a moat (brand, cost advantage, network, switching costs, licence) visible in persistently high returns on capital? Are owner earnings (cash after maintenance capex) growing? Is management candid and are capital-allocation decisions rational (buybacks, dividends, acquisitions)? Invert: what would make this a bad investment in 10 years? *BIST:* check real ROE vs inflation, related-party leakage to the controlling group, and FX debt.

**Fisher — scuttlebutt growth.** Does the company have products/markets with years of growth ahead, effective R&D and sales, improving margins, depth of management, honest communication in bad times, and growth without heavy dilution? *BIST:* read activity reports, capacity announcements (KAP), export share and customer concentration.

**Lynch — know the category and the story.** Which type is it: slow grower, stalwart, fast grower, cyclical, turnaround, asset play? Does the valuation fit the type (e.g. PEG near or below 1 for growers, cycle position for cyclicals)? Can you state the story in two sentences and check it every quarter? *BIST:* cyclicals (steel, chemicals, autos, airlines) need the cycle view; asset plays (holdings, REITs/GYO) need NAV discounts and governance.

**Greenblatt — good and cheap together.** Rank by return on capital and by earnings yield (EBIT / enterprise value); prefer names high on both; hold a diversified basket. *BIST:* exclude banks/insurers/holdings; use TTM EBIT with care under TMS 29.

**Damodaran — narrative and numbers.** Does the story translate into consistent revenue growth, margins, reinvestment and risk? Are cash flows and discount rates in the same currency and inflation basis (TRY with TRY rates, USD with USD rates plus Türkiye country risk premium)? What must be true for today's price (reverse DCF)?

**Accounting forensics.** Piotroski F-score (profitability, leverage/liquidity, efficiency trend), Altman Z for distress, Beneish-style warning signs (receivables growing faster than sales, rising accruals, declining asset quality), cash conversion (CFO ÷ net income). *BIST:* monetary gain/loss under TMS 29 and FX gains can dominate profit — look at operating cash flow.

## Growth, trend and timing

**O'Neil — CAN SLIM.** Strong recent quarterly and annual earnings growth, something new (product, management, high), supply/demand evident in volume, a leader not a laggard (high relative strength), institutional sponsorship, and — most important — a market in a confirmed uptrend (follow-through day, few distribution days). *BIST:* market direction first; `market-regime-analysis` supplies it.

**Minervini — trend template and tight bases.** Price above rising 150/200-day averages, 50-day above both, well above the 52-week low and near the high, strong relative strength; entries from volatility contraction patterns with volume dry-up; cut losses quickly and keep losses much smaller than gains. *BIST:* ±10% daily limits and gaps mean stops need slack and smaller size.

**Weinstein — stage analysis.** Buy stage 2 (price above a rising 30-week average after a base), avoid stage 4 (below a falling average), be careful in stage 3 tops. *BIST:* check the USD-based chart too; TL inflation can disguise a stage 4 in real terms.

**Livermore / Darvas — pivots and boxes.** Enter when price clears a well-defined pivot or box top with volume; add only to winners; never average down on a losing trade.

**Druckenmiller / Soros — liquidity and asymmetry.** Is central-bank liquidity turning in our favour? Is there a large, asymmetric payoff with a clear stop? Reflexivity: are price moves themselves changing fundamentals (e.g. capital raises at high prices)? *BIST:* TCMB policy path, real rates, carry flows and foreign-investor share matter more than for mature markets.

## Risk and judgement

**Marks — second-level thinking and cycles.** What does the consensus believe, and why might it be wrong? Where are we in the market cycle (fear/greed, credit conditions)? Is the risk of permanent loss acceptable?

**Taleb — fragility.** Does the position survive the worst plausible day (limit-down gap, trading halt, VBTS measure, FX shock)? Avoid exposures with unbounded or hidden tails; keep a barbell of safe capital and small speculative bets when appropriate.

**Thorp / Kelly — sizing.** With an honest probability and payoff, the growth-optimal fraction is modest; use a fraction of Kelly (¼–½) as a ceiling, never as a target, and never with a fragile edge estimate.

**Tetlock / Mauboussin — calibrated forecasting.** Start from base rates (how often do stocks with this setup/valuation beat the market over this horizon?), then adjust for specifics; express probabilities, update in small steps, keep score in the forecast ledger. Separate skill from luck.
