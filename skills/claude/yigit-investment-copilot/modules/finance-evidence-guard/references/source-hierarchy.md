# Source hierarchy

## Preferred order

1. Regulator, exchange, central bank, statistics office, government agency, or legally responsible issuer/fund manager.
2. Audited filing, prospectus, investor-relations release, official holdings file, or official product terms.
3. Licensed market-data provider with clear timestamp and coverage.
4. Reputable research or news source that names its evidence.
5. Aggregator, unofficial API, scraper, social media, forum, or model-generated text.

Use lower tiers for discovery or corroboration, not to override a stronger primary source without an explained reason.

## Two-source rule

Seek an independent second source for executable price, corporate action, fee, tax/legal interpretation, fund holdings, major catalyst, or any fact that directly changes quantity or action. A primary source plus one independent market-data source is normally stronger than two aggregators sharing the same upstream feed.

## Conflict checks

Before calling two values inconsistent, compare:

- Instrument and share class
- Exchange and trading session
- Currency and FX timestamp
- Reporting period and publication vintage
- Consolidated versus standalone statements
- Gross versus net, nominal versus real, and adjusted versus unadjusted
- Last trade, bid, ask, indicative NAV, official NAV, or close
