# Investing playbook: what the books and the research agree on

A synthesis in our own words of classic investing and trading books and academic finance, adapted to Borsa İstanbul. Use it to reason; cite the underlying evidence, not this file, when a claim matters.

## 1. The honest core

- Short-term prices are close to a random walk: most daily and weekly moves are noise. No method removes the chance of loss; the aim is a positive expected value across many decisions plus survival through the bad ones.
- Edge comes from a few places: (a) persistent risk/behavioural premia (value, momentum, quality, low volatility), (b) better information processing on events (reading filings faster and better, not inside information), (c) discipline — costs, sizing, exits, avoiding big mistakes — and (d) patience when others must sell.
- The biggest controllable drivers of results are: avoiding large losses, position sizing, costs/turnover and behaviour. Stock picking skill comes after these.

## 2. Return arithmetic

- Losses are asymmetric: −20% needs +25%, −50% needs +100%. Cutting losses early protects compounding.
- Expectancy per trade in R: `E = p(win)·avg_win_R − p(loss)·avg_loss_R`. A 40% hit rate with 3R winners and 1R losers is strongly positive; a 70% hit rate with small wins and occasional big losses can be negative.
- Kelly sizing maximises growth only with a known edge; with estimated edges use a small fraction (¼–½ Kelly) as a ceiling.
- Costs compound: a 0.4% round trip ten times a year is 4% a year before any mistake.
- In Türkiye nominal TL returns must be compared with inflation, deposit/money-market returns and USD returns; a +40% TL year can be a real loss.

## 3. Evidence map (strength for decision-making)

| Idea | Evidence | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| Equity risk premium over long horizons | Strong globally; volatile in Türkiye in USD terms | Long-term equity exposure sized to the ability to hold through drawdowns |
| Value (cheap vs fundamentals) | Strong long-run, long droughts; present in BIST studies | Value lane; avoid value traps with quality/cash-flow checks |
| Momentum (12-1 month winners) | Strong in developed markets; mixed/weak historically on BIST | Blend with other lanes; test before tilting; crash risk at regime turns |
| Short-term reversal (1 month) | Documented; stronger in less liquid stocks; found in early ISE data | Reversal lane for short horizons, quality-gated |
| Quality / profitability (Novy-Marx; Piotroski) | Strong, defensive | Quality lane and accounting forensics |
| Low volatility / low beta (Frazzini & Pedersen) | Robust, defensive in drawdowns | Low-risk lane; sizing |
| Post-earnings-announcement drift (Bernard & Thomas) | Documented, decaying in large caps | Treat strong surprises as catalysts; read filings quickly |
| 52-week-high proximity (George & Hwang) | Documented | Part of momentum and trend template |
| Insider buying / company buybacks | Moderate positive evidence; context-dependent | Catalyst lane; check size and who sells |
| Chart patterns and indicators alone | Weak/mixed after costs (Aronson's data-mining critique; Bulkowski's pattern statistics show wide failure rates) | Timing and risk placement, not prediction |
| News/social sentiment | Short-lived, crowded, prone to manipulation | Event discovery only |
| LLM "predictions" | Look-ahead and memorisation bias in evaluations | Never trust without point-in-time tests |

Published anomalies shrink after publication and many fail replication (McLean & Pontiff; Hou, Xue & Zhang; Harvey, Liu & Zhu). Expect less than the papers show.

## 4. Horizon playbooks

**Days to 4 weeks (swing):** market regime and liquidity first; setups with clear invalidation (breakout from a tight base, pullback in an uptrend, oversold quality); catalysts dated inside the window; size for gaps (±10% limits); fast exits; costs matter most.

**1–6 months (position):** earnings direction and revisions, valuation vs peers, sector/macro tailwinds (TCMB path, FX, commodity), relative strength, a visible catalyst path; review after each report.

**1+ years (investment):** business quality (real returns on capital above the cost of capital), balance sheet, capital allocation and governance, reinvestment runway, price vs value with a margin of safety; technicals only for entry pacing. Compare with index funds and TEFAS alternatives.

## 5. Türkiye realities to keep in mind

- **Inflation and TMS 29:** restated statements, monetary gains/losses, nominal growth illusions.
- **Currency:** many listed firms earn in FX or pay costs in FX; check net FX position and export share.
- **High risk-free rate:** deposits/money-market funds are a serious alternative; a stock must offer more after risk.
- **Foreign flows and politics:** liquid large caps react first to global risk-off and domestic political/legal shocks; breadth collapses quickly.
- **Retail-dominated small caps:** limit-up streaks, bedelsiz front-running, Telegram hype and pump-and-dump footprints; SPK and Borsa İstanbul measures (VBTS) often follow.
- **IPOs:** strong early momentum and frequent later underperformance; lock-up expiries and supply matter.
- **Index composition:** banks and holdings weigh heavily; index moves can hide weak breadth.
- **Taxes and costs:** 0% withholding on BIST share gains for residents at the last check, 15% on dividends; commissions and BSMV vary by broker.

## 6. Behaviour: the enemy inside (Kahneman & Tversky, Thaler, Odean, Shefrin & Statman)

Overconfidence (too much trading), confirmation bias (reading only bullish news), anchoring (on the purchase price or an old target), recency and herding (chasing last month's winners), loss aversion and the disposition effect (selling winners, holding losers), sunk-cost thinking, and story bias. Counter with: written plans before orders, pre-mortems, bear case first, checklists, fixed risk per trade, stop rules decided in advance, and a journal reviewed by rules not by feelings.

## 7. Forecasting discipline (Tetlock; Mauboussin)

Start from base rates; adjust for specifics with evidence; express probabilities and ranges; update gradually; keep score (forecast ledger: coverage, Brier, benchmark). Separate skill from luck: judge process quality over many decisions, not single outcomes.

## 8. Risk management principles (Thorp, Taleb, Marks, Tharp)

Survive first: no single idea should be able to cause ruin. Size from the stop and the gap risk; cap portfolio heat; diversify across drivers, not tickers; remember correlations jump in crises; keep cash as an option; avoid leverage you do not fully understand; prefer asymmetric bets with bounded downside.

## 9. The system loop

Regime → broad scan → shortlist → deep dive (fundamentals, events, technicals, flow) → committee (bear first) → red team → pre-trade gate → written plan → user executes → monitor (stops, events, regime) → exit by rules → journal and ledger → quarterly process review → change at most one or two rules at a time.

## 10. Reading list (one-line takeaways)

- Graham — *The Intelligent Investor*: margin of safety; Mr. Market serves you, he does not guide you.
- Buffett's shareholder letters; Munger's talks: durable moats, rational capital allocation, inversion.
- Fisher — *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits*: scuttlebutt; long growth runways.
- Lynch — *One Up on Wall Street*: know what you own and which category it is.
- Greenblatt — *The Little Book That Beats the Market*: good + cheap, systematically.
- Damodaran — valuation books and country-risk data: narrative and numbers, currency consistency.
- Marks — *The Most Important Thing*: second-level thinking and cycles.
- O'Neil — *How to Make Money in Stocks*: CAN SLIM, market direction first, cut losses.
- Minervini — *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard*: trend template, tight bases, small losses.
- Weinstein — *Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets*: stage analysis.
- Murphy — *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets* and *Intermarket Analysis*: tools and cross-asset links.
- Aronson — *Evidence-Based Technical Analysis*: data-mining bias; test before believing.
- Tharp — *Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom*: R-multiples, expectancy, position sizing.
- Douglas — *Trading in the Zone*: think in probabilities, execute consistently.
- Kahneman — *Thinking, Fast and Slow*; Thaler — *Misbehaving*: biases.
- Tetlock & Gardner — *Superforecasting*: calibration and scoring.
- Mauboussin — *Expectations Investing*, *The Success Equation*: reverse DCF, skill vs luck.
- Taleb — *Fooled by Randomness*, *Antifragile*: tails, fragility, survival.
- López de Prado — *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*: purged CV, meta-labeling, deflated Sharpe.
- Thorp — *A Man for All Markets*; Poundstone — *Fortune's Formula*: Kelly sizing and its limits.
