# Horizon-specific factors

## Intraday to four weeks

Prioritize liquidity/spread, dated catalysts, event gap risk, volatility regime, technical structure, flow/positioning evidence when available, and balance-sheet/disclosure red flags. Use fundamentals as a risk and catalyst filter. Shrink directional forecasts heavily; do not pretend DCF predicts next week.

## One to twelve months

Balance earnings revisions, operating momentum, sector/macro transmission, valuation, catalysts, quality, cash flow, balance sheet, technical regime and positioning. Require at least one pathway for the market to recognize the thesis within the horizon.

## More than twelve months

Prioritize business economics, competitive advantage, reinvestment runway, ROIC, free cash flow, balance sheet, capital allocation, dilution, governance and valuation. Technicals mainly shape entry and risk, not the long-run thesis.

## High-risk opportunity lane

Rank probability-weighted upside, catalyst quality, convexity, liquidity/spread, severe-loss probability and invalidation quality. Distinguish highest theoretical payoff from highest realistic expected value. A full-loss budget does not remove the need for a positive or explicitly lottery-like setup.

## BIST evidence note

Early Borsa İstanbul studies found short-to-medium-term contrarian (reversal) profits rather than US-style momentum; later studies find size, value and profitability factors informative. Do not assume momentum works on BIST: the `kisa` scan profile keeps a reversal lane, the `orta` profile blends momentum with value/quality/growth, and weights change only with walk-forward evidence from `quant-research-lab`. In high-inflation periods compare nominal TL returns with CPI, deposit rates and USD-based returns.

## Sector adaptation

Use sector-specific metrics. Banks, insurers, REITs, commodity producers, software, industrials and early-stage businesses cannot be ranked fairly with one universal accounting ratio set.
