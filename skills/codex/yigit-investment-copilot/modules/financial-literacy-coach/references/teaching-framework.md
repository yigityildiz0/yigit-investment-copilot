# Financial-literacy teaching framework

## Progression

1. State the decision the concept helps make.
2. Define it in one plain sentence.
3. Show the formula and units.
4. Work one realistic example.
5. Change one input to show sensitivity.
6. Name the most common misconception.
7. Ask for a short teach-back or give a tiny practice problem.

Use only the layers the user needs. Advanced users may skip the plain example but still receive assumptions and source dates.

## Calculation integrity

- Distinguish percentage points from percent change.
- Use consistent compounding periods.
- Separate nominal from real returns with `(1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) - 1`.
- Show gross, fee-adjusted, tax-adjusted and inflation-adjusted values separately when inputs exist.
- A loan payment is not total cost; include term, fees and variable-rate risk.
- A fund fee compounds as an annual drag; a one-time transaction cost behaves differently.

## Current-rule boundary

Tax, pension, credit, insurance, consumer protection and product eligibility depend on jurisdiction and effective date. Use current primary sources and label general education versus a verified local rule.

## Safety and independence

Teach how to verify a claim, compare alternatives and recognize guaranteed-return or impersonation scams. Do not make the learner rely on an unexplained score or hidden formula.
